**1. Interpretable classifier**

The two classes in the training data are easily distinguished by the *horizontal connectivity* of the black pixels.

| Class | Typical shape | Key feature |
|-------|---------------|-------------|
| **0** | A closed loop (circle/oval). | Every scan‑line (row) contains **one single contiguous block** of black pixels. |
| **1** | Two vertical bars joined by one or more horizontal bars. | Some scan‑lines contain **two separate blocks** of black pixels – the two vertical bars – which are joined by horizontal bars that create a gap between the blocks. |

**Decision rule**

1. For each image, scan all rows (y‑coordinates) from top to bottom.  
2. Count how many rows contain **two or more disjoint black‑pixel segments**.  
3. If that count is **zero** → predict **class 0**.  
4. If the count is **> 0** → predict **class 1**.

This rule is fully interpretable: it simply checks whether any horizontal line in the image splits the black pixels into two separate parts, which is the hallmark of the “two‑bar” shape of class 1.

---

**2. Decision rules explained**

* **Class 0 (circle)** – The black pixels form a single closed loop.  
  *Every* horizontal line cuts the loop in one continuous piece; there is no row that shows two separate black blocks.  
  → Rule 3 applies → class 0.

* **Class 1 (two vertical bars with horizontal connectors)** – The black pixels are arranged in two vertical columns.  
  In many rows the two columns are separate, producing two disjoint black blocks.  
  When a horizontal bar connects the columns, that row shows a single block, but the presence of any row with two blocks is enough to identify the shape.  
  → Rule 4 applies → class 1.
