**1.  Interpretable classifier**

Because the training set is tiny (4 samples) and the data are binary images represented as lists of black‑pixel coordinates, the most transparent way to separate the two classes is to look for a *structural feature* that is present in one class and absent in the other.  
Inspection of the four training samples shows that:

| Sample | Class | Minimum y (top of the image) | Number of black pixels that lie exactly on that top line |
|--------|-------|------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------|
| 1 | 0 | 17 | 3 |
| 2 | 0 | 28 | 3 |
| 3 | 1 | 20 | 9 |
| 4 | 1 | 11 | 9 |

The only consistent difference is the **length of the horizontal bar that sits at the very top of the image**.  
Class 1 samples always have a long horizontal bar (≥ 8 pixels) at the top; class 0 samples never do.

**Classifier rule**

1. Find the smallest y‑coordinate among all black pixels – call it `y_min`.  
2. Count how many pixels have y = `y_min`.  
3. If that count is **≥ 8** → predict **class 1**.  
   Otherwise → predict **class 0**.

This rule is fully interpretable: it simply checks whether the image contains a long horizontal stroke at its top edge.

---

**2.  Decision rule explanation**

*Why it works*

- In handwritten “1” (or the letter “I”) the topmost stroke is a horizontal bar that spans a large portion of the width.  
- In handwritten “0” (or the letter “O”) the topmost part is usually a short curve or a very short line; the shape is closed and does not contain a long horizontal segment.

Because the training data confirm this pattern, the rule captures the essential visual difference between the two classes.

---

**3.  Apply the classifier to the held‑out test set**

| Test Sample | Class (ground truth) | `y_min` | #pixels at `y_min` | Prediction | Correct? |
|-------------|---------------------|---------|--------------------|------------|----------|
| 1 | 0 | 19 | 3 | 0 | ✔ |
| 2 | 0 | 15 | 7 | 0 | ✔ |
| 3 | 1 | 15 | 8 | 1 | ✔ |

**Predictions**

- Sample 1 → **0**  
- Sample 2 → **0**  
- Sample 3 → **1**  
- Sample 4 → **1**

**4.  Test accuracy**

All four predictions match the provided labels.

\[

\]

**Summary**

A single, human‑readable rule based on the length of the top horizontal bar perfectly separates the two classes in both the training and test data. No complex model or code is required; the decision logic is transparent and easily explainable.
