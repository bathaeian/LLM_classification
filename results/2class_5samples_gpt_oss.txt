**1.  Interpret­able classifier**

Because the training images are very small (105 × 105) and the two classes
look qualitatively different, the most natural “hand‑crafted” descriptor is
a *topological* one: the number of *internal holes* that the binary image
contains.  
For a 2‑bit image that is perfectly separable with this idea.  
The mathematical quantity that captures this is the **Euler number**

\[
E = C_{\text{black}} \;-\; H ,
\]

where \(C_{\text{black}}\) is the number of connected components made up of
black pixels and \(H\) is the number of enclosed white regions (holes).

* **Class 0** – the samples look like the handwritten digit “0” or “O”.  
  They have *exactly one* black connected component that encloses a
  single white interior, hence \(C_{\text{black}} = 1\) and \(H = 1\) so

  \[
  E = 1-1 = 0 .
  \]

* **Class’int** – the samples look like the handwritten digit “1”.  
  They consist only of a single black bar with no nested white region,
  therefore \(C_{\text{black}} = 1\) and \(H = 0\) giving

  \[
  E = 1-0 = 1 .
  \]

**Decision rule**

| Euler number | Predicted class |
|--------------|-----------------|
| 0            | 0 (symbol with a hole) |
| 1            | 1 (vertical stroke)   |

Only two arithmetic operations (counting components and holes) are needed,
so the rule is fully interpretable.
