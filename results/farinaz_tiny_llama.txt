### Task 1: Propose an Interpretable Classifier

Given the nature of the data, which consists of handwritten characters represented by the coordinates of black pixels on a grid, a suitable classifier could be a decision tree or a random forest classifier. These classifiers are interpretable as they provide a clear set of rules (decisions based on feature values) that lead to the classification of a sample.

For the sake of simplicity and interpretability, let's propose a decision tree classifier. Decision trees work by recursively partitioning the data into smaller subsets based on the feature values. In this case, features could include:

- The density of points in certain regions of the image.
- The presence or absence of points in specific areas that are characteristic of certain letters.
- The shape and connectivity of the points, which could indicate strokes or lines typical of handwritten characters.

### Task 2: Explain the Decision Rules

The decision rules for a decision tree classifier would be based on the features extracted from the dataset. For handwritten characters, some potential decision rules could include:

1. **Presence of a Vertical Stroke:** If a significant number of points are vertically aligned in the middle of the image, it could indicate the character 'B', 'D', or possibly 'A' if considering uppercase letters.
2. **Circular or Oval Shape:** A high density of points forming a circular or oval shape could indicate the characters 'O', 'Q', 'C', or 'G'.
3. **Diagonal or Slanted Stroke:** The presence of points forming a diagonal or slanted line could suggest characters like 'K', 'X', or 'V'.
4. **Horizontal Stroke at the Top:** Characters like 'T' or 'E' could be identified by a horizontal line of points at the top of the image.
5. **Hook or Tail:** The presence of a hook or a tail at the bottom of the character could distinguish 'J' from 'L'.

These are simplified examples, and actual decision rules would depend on the complexity and variability of the handwritten characters in the training dataset.

### Task 3: Apply the Classifier to the Held-Out Test Dataset

To apply the decision tree classifier to the test dataset, we would feed the features extracted from each test sample into the trained decision tree model. The model would then traverse the tree based on the feature values of each sample, applying the decision rules until it reaches a leaf node that corresponds to a specific class label.
